The data belongs to the law firm’s clients. How do you build and test an agent when you can’t see its work? Jacob Lauritzen is the CTO of Legora, one of the fastest-growing companies in legal tech history. The team started in a Stockholm basement with ChatGPT, Swedish law, and a search tool. Today, a lawyer can use Legora to draft and edit Word documents, review 10,000 contracts in a single table, or run due diligence. The agent also coordinates work between lawyers, clients, and other agents. When Legora made its own acquisitions, the team used it for diligence. What we talked about • Why Legora resisted adding a model picker for years, and why it finally added one • Jacob’s estimate that models already do about 60% of legal work well, and why the rest takes a smarter model • How three engineers and three lawyers build test environments modeled on customers’ work, much like the “worlds” used in reinforcement learning • A citation problem where the model got it right 70% of the time, then 99% after one release. Legora didn’t have to change anything. • Why Legora runs everything on Azure and why it doesn’t use ElevenLabs • Tabular Review, the million-cell spreadsheet the industry copied down to the name • How intake works, how agents create lists, and how a list can start another agent • What legal engineers and forward-deployed engineers each do, and who gets to write code • The 300 Spartans slide Jacob used to argue that 20 engineers would be enough forever.
