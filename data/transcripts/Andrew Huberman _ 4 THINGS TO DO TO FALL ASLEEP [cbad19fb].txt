[SHOW NOTES]
Sleep is the foundation of mental health, physical health, and performance. Each of us has different sleep needs but most need between 6-9 hours.

The longer we are awake, the more adenosine builds up in our body and brain. Adenosine promote sleepiness.

Many things inhibit our ability to fall asleep. Caffeine too close to bedtime, room too hot, gnawing hunger, worry etc.

This video explains 4 key things that promote falling asleep. These are things you can control.

1) Core body temperature needs to drop.
The reason a hot bath, shower, or warm compress to the upper face work to lower core body temperature is, after you get out, the medial preoptic nucleus of your hypothalamus, which contains neurons that are sort of like a thermostat, trigger a drop in your core body temp. No hot water? Holding something cool (not ice cold) can help too.

2) Long exhales until your lungs are empty slows your heart down. This is actually the basis of HRV. It’s controlled by a descending branch of your vagus nerve.

3) It’s impossible to turn off your thoughts, but you can shift thinking about the past or plans or worries to your immediate sensations at the level of your skin inward (interoception).

4) “Rocking” slowly back-and-forth triggers reflexive eye movements that in turn help falling asleep. It’s because those eye movements turn off a set of circuits that pay attention to limb position. But most don’t have beds that allow them to be slowly rocked!

By slowly moving your eyes from side to side (underneath closed eye eyelids), & up and down, many people find they fall (back) asleep. Indeed there are eyemasks being developed that trigger this pattern of eye movements to induce sleep. In the meantime, you can try this trick. Many people find it works. It may not work for you, but it costs nothing and is safe.
